Needs all of — the skills a person must hold to take this post properly. Tick two and it takes one person holding both.

Fallback cover — used when nobody qualified for the post is free. Normally someone already on duty that shift holding the fallback skill absorbs it as extra duty. These show as * on the roster and are counted separately.

Emergency fallback call-in — permits a person holding the fallback skill to be rostered as an extra person solely to cover this post when normal on-duty cover is impossible. Hard rules still apply: no booked leave, no day + night on the same date, correct shift eligibility, no approved leave, and no breaking a strict block/off day. Enabled by default only for night Pharmacy, so a pharmacy-cover-trained nurse may be called in as the extra night nurse.

Can double up — a person on this post may also hold another double-up post in the same shift as a normal assignment.

Keep full strength — if someone on this post ends up covering elsewhere, roster an extra head so the required number stays free. Ticked on nursing, day and night: two nurses stay on the ward even when a third is carrying pharmacy, because one person cannot hold ward, casualty and pharmacy at once.

Shift adjustment — tick this for a small department that covers for itself. Their block still runs as all days or all nights, but the off day that ends it can move when a colleague is away. Ticked on reception, laboratory and housekeeping; nursing and pharmacy keep the strict cycle.

Adjust on the day — tick this where a shortfall is handled by moving someone around on the day rather than by the roster. Those shifts are listed for you as actions instead of counted as failures, and the roster will not break a cycle chasing them. Ticked on reception (day), laboratory, housekeeping and pharmacy.

Cover is normal — tick this only if you formally approve fallback cover as a routine arrangement. The default Pharmacy cover is treated as an exception and shown in the coverage report.

Minimum = hard floor. The roster will not be generated or printed if any post falls below its configured minimum on any date or shift. Preferred heads may be higher than minimum; Day housekeeping still prefers 2 and accepts 1.

Must stay free — how many people on this post must be left with no extra duty. Set to 1 on nursing, so a nurse only ever covers pharmacy when a second nurse is on that shift to hold the ward and casualty. With one nurse rostered, no cover is possible and the shortfall is reported instead.

NS exemption — enabled for Reception 3 by default. The planner reduces this person’s NS duties and excludes them from peer-load comparisons. Emergency only — use this for standby/cover staff. They are excluded from the full-utilisation cycle and receive duty only when an enabled fallback post actually needs them.

04

Period & rules

Every one of these is a dial.
Flexible scheduling plans four ordinary off dates per calendar month.

Always enforced: nobody gets a DS/ES post and an NS post on the same date; nobody is rostered on a date marked leave; nobody is placed on a post whose skills they don't hold.

Flexible blocks — blocks may be shorter. At most six consecutive working days and five consecutive nights are allowed. A DS/ES-to-NS change may occur inside a work stretch; the total consecutive-work count continues across that change. NS-to-DS/ES changes require an off day. A dated management approval may allow one extra duty, followed by an off day.

NS fairness — comparable staff in each department must have equal NS totals, or differ by at most one NS duty per calendar month. No staff member may exceed 10 NS duties in a calendar-month roster. NS-exempt, fixed-shift, resident and Emergency-only staff remain excluded from peer comparison, but the 10-night hard maximum still applies. Generate one calendar month at a time; use weekly printing for weekly rosters.

Shorter blocks — NS blocks may contain one to five NS duties. A real off date is required before returning to DS/ES duty.

Exemptions — fixed-shift staff keep their eligibility. Receptionist 3 remains NS-exempt from the fairness comparison and receives the lowest NS target found by the search.

Peer-load fairness — after hard coverage and safety rules, the planner minimises the difference between the highest and lowest day-side (DS+ES) totals and NS totals among comparable rotating staff in each department. NS spread must be 0–1 per month in every comparable group, including Nursing. day-side (DS+ES) totals are also balanced when leave and eligibility permit. Exempt staff are not used to judge fairness.

Four-off rule — four ordinary off dates are planned per month, including ordinary requested leave. Approved additional leave is separate (AL). Worked dates never reset the consecutive-work counter. If availability or prior-month work makes these rules incompatible, generation reports a conflict.

Nursing — six nurses receive balanced NS totals with flexible blocks. Mondays and Fridays still require at least three day-side nurses. On any date with more than two nurses on the day side, exactly one is assigned ES (12:00 PM–10:00 PM); the others remain DS (8:00 AM–6:00 PM). ES counts as one normal 10-hour duty. The two nurses with Pharmacy-cover skill are kept on different rostered shifts; if both must work on the day side under the 10-NS monthly cap, one is assigned ES. NS Nursing prefers two; one is the emergency minimum and cannot take extra cover.

Pharmacy coverage — hard rule — three regular Pharmacy staff are scheduled first. Trained nurse cover is used only if a Pharmacy worker cannot cover that shift, and each occurrence is identified. An uncovered Pharmacy shift blocks the roster.

Locked minimum: Pharmacy requires 1 person on DS/ES and 1 person on NS. This floor cannot be reduced below 1 in Setup; a roster with 0 Pharmacy staff on either shift is rejected.
